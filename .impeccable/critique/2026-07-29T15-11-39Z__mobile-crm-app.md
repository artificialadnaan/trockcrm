## Design Specificity Verdict

**Authored for T Rock in the newest ~15% of the surface area. The rest would survive a logo swap into any CRM.**

**LLM assessment.** The evidence of a real point of view is concentrated and genuine. `theme.ts:1-19` states a brief no generic system would state — *"used on roofs, in trucks and in job trailers by people wearing gloves. It should read as equipment, not as a web dashboard someone shrank"* — and the palette is derived from the mark rather than from a default light theme. `theme.ts:39-51,123-132` splits **`brandRed` (a fill, 3.7:1 on near-black) from `redText` (type, 7.9:1)**, which is a systems insight rather than a colour pick: it is why the brand can appear in a call button, a back chevron, a retry label and the 11px active tab without any of them failing contrast. The pure-black chrome with a 2px brand-red bottom rule does more identity work than tinting a surface would. `BoardCard.tsx:41-46` encodes at-risk/on-hold as a 3px rail *because* an 11px chip is not legible in peripheral vision in sunlight. And the copy is unfakeable: *"nobody in, walked the roof, wants a quote"*, *"Undercut on price, went with an incumbent"*, *"Same building · 12 m away — check the suite"*.

Against that: the dashboard is the category default (greeting, one big number, a 2×2 icon-over-label grid of stock Ionicons — a third icon set, where `DESIGN.md` says Lucide and iOS says SF Symbols). The type scale authored to fix "nothing on screen leads" reaches four files. `inkNavy` is a live shim pointing a colour named for navy at `#F5F7FA`.

**Is the dark system defensible divergence or drift?** Defensible, with an undocumented mandate. The reasoning in `theme.ts` is sound and `theme-contrast.test.ts` pins pairs to **real call sites rather than intentions** — its own comment, *"a contrast test that checks the combinations you meant to ship is a test of your intentions,"* is the most useful sentence in the repo. But the mandate lives only in code comments, and two of `DESIGN.md`'s named Don'ts are now violated with no written override.

**Deterministic scan — a null result, not a clean bill.** `detect.mjs --json` over `app/`, `src/components/` and `src/theme/` returned `[]`, exit 0. Also `[]` with `--no-config`, per-directory, and per-file. **That zero is not evidence of quality, and Assessment B proved it with two controls:** a `.tsx` fixture written in web CSS-in-JS idiom fired 2 rules and exited 2; **the same violations re-expressed in React Native `StyleSheet.create` idiom** — `borderLeftWidth: 4` + `borderLeftColor` split across two keys, `fontSize: 9`, `letterSpacing: -1.2`, `textTransform: "uppercase"`, a 28pt touch target — fired **nothing** and exited 0. The detector's engine keys on CSS declaration syntax (`prop: <value><unit>`, shorthand strings, `cubic-bezier`, `linear-gradient`); RN's numeric object form is invisible to it.

Triaged against RN, of the 60 registered rules: **~28 are false positives by construction** (9 need a live DOM via Puppeteer and never executed; the rest assume `<h1>`–`<h6>` semantics, `ch` units, CSS gradients/keyframes, or CSS custom properties). **~11 are untranslated** — the concern is real but the RN expression differs, and three of those would have fired had they been translated: `tiny-text`/`undersized-ui-text` (151 raw `fontSize` literals), `side-tab` (`BoardCard.tsx:155` `rail: { width: 3 }`, invisible because RN splits the declaration in two), and `overused-font` (the app is on Inter, an explicitly named overused face, but RN writes it as `Inter_400Regular`). **Zero rules genuinely apply and pass.** Nine browser-only rules are **unknown**, not clean.

So Assessment B's real contribution here is the hand-built evidence, not the scan.

**Visual overlays:** none. No browser surface exists, no live server was started, no overlay was injected or claimed.

## Overall Impression

This app is unusually honest and unusually inconsistent, and the two are related. The recent work — `prospect.tsx`, `board.tsx`, `BoardCard`, `Badge`, the theme, the failure-state taxonomy — was done by someone reasoning from the job site, and it shows in decisions no template produces. The older screens were never brought forward, so the product now contains two generations of standards side by side: 44pt targets on `prospect.tsx` and ~18pt bare-text back links on `deals/[id].tsx`; a 7-step type scale on four files and 15 ad-hoc sizes on twenty-two.

The single biggest opportunity is not new design. It is **back-application** — the rules were already learned and written down, and they stopped at the file boundary of whatever PR learned them.

The second-biggest is the gap between how well this app *words* reassurance and how little *weight* it gives it. Almost every message is well-written; almost none is announced, scrolled to, or typographically distinguished from a "Change" link.

## What's Working

**1. The failure-state taxonomy is the real design achievement.** `resolveListState` + `RetryNotice`/`RetryBlock` draw a line most products never draw — "we have data and the refresh failed" renders differently from "we never got data" — and the retry is placed where the user's eyes actually are. `RetryNotice.tsx:5-18` reasons it out: a failed pull-to-refresh belongs at the *top*, because the user is still at the top; a failed load-more belongs at the *bottom*, because that is where they hit it. That is a design decision expressed as a component API (`placement: "top" | "bottom"`), and it is why a rep on a truck's connection never loses the screen they were reading. Industry norm is a full-screen error that discards a loaded list.

**2. Token discipline on colour and spacing is genuinely clean — better than Assessment A assumed.** Measured: **zero hard-coded colours in any style value** across `app/` and `src/components/` (the 7 hex literals that exist are all inside comments; one of them, `#721`, is a PR number), against 313 `theme.color.*` references. Spacing: **13 raw numeric literals**, all values 1–6 hairlines and nudges, against **260** `theme.space.*` references. Zero `fontWeight:` anywhere. The unadopted layer is *specifically the type scale* — which makes it a bounded, mechanical migration rather than a general drift problem.

**3. The prospect screen's copy refuses to promise things that don't exist.** `prospect.tsx:2016-2021`: *"Marked as worth a lead. Follow up with the office — this doesn't reach them on its own yet."* An app that flagged a lead and said nothing would have been easier to ship and would have quietly burned the rep's trust the first time nothing happened. Same discipline at `prospect.tsx:1257` ("link it on the web rather than adding another") and `leads/[id].tsx:436` (refusing to repeat the server's "inline fix" hint, because this screen has no editor). That honesty *is* "No-BS" executed as product behaviour.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] The `locating` state removes every escape hatch for up to 12 seconds

**What.** `prospect.tsx:1128-1132` swaps the entire property card for a spinner. The company-fallback link lives inside the `idle` branch only (`1119-1126`); the retry inside `unavailable` only (`1145-1152`); the whole fallback block is gated on `matches?.length === 0 || rejectedMatches || denied || unavailable || matchError` (`1287-1293`), none of which is true while locating. Save is independently blocked (`910`). Timeout is `LOCATION_FIX_TIMEOUT_MS = 12_000`.

**Why it matters.** This is the exact scene the screen was built for: standing at a door, one bar, GPS confused by a metal roof. The rep's only option for twelve seconds is to leave the screen and lose what they typed. The comment at `1114-1118` already argues that "GPS is not always the point" — and the implementation then makes GPS the only point for twelve seconds.

**Fix.** Hoist the `setRejectedMatches(true)` Pressable out of the `idle` ternary so it renders as a sibling of the whole status block, in every location state. Add a "Stop looking" control calling `location.reset()`. Either drop `location.state.status === "locating"` from `saveBlocked` once a fallback target is chosen, or keep the block and make the fallback reachable — which is the point.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P1] 45 of 87 interactive elements compute below 44×44pt, and the split is by file age

**What.** AST-resolved across all 35 `.tsx` files (heights are static estimates: padding + `lineHeight`, or `fontSize × 1.2` where unset).

- **~18pt, no style and no `hitSlop`:** back links at `contacts/[id].tsx:132`, `deals/[id].tsx:197`, `move.tsx:318`; the tap-to-call / tap-to-email row at `deals/[id].tsx:459` (~17pt, `flexDirection: "row"` with no vertical padding).
- **~34pt with `hitSlop={8}`:** `stage/[stageId].tsx:79`, `leads/[id].tsx:276`.
- **31.6–34pt, 20 elements** on `paddingVertical: theme.space.sm` — including **`move.tsx:390`, `:469`, `:554`: the stage picker, the close-date picker and the lost-reason picker, i.e. the entire primary interaction of the Move screen**.
- **A contact with no job title and no company renders a ~19pt row** (`contacts/index.tsx:226`).

Meanwhile `minHeight` appears 20 times in **only three files** — `prospect.tsx` (18 controls, all explicit 44/48/52/56), `board.tsx`, `leads/[id].tsx` — plus `RetryBlock.tsx`. `board.tsx:352` even comments on the HIG minimum.

**Why it matters.** The stated user wears gloves for half the year. The rule was learned on the new screens and never back-applied to the ones opened most.

**Fix.** Add `minHeight: 44` to the shared row/pill/back styles rather than per call site, and add an AST test asserting every `Pressable` resolves to ≥44 or carries compensating `hitSlop` — the same guard shape already used in the other app.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable adapt`

### [P1] The app announces less than it renders — and one comment asserts the opposite of the platform's behaviour

Three findings, one cluster.

**a. `textTransform: "uppercase"` with no `accessibilityLabel` — 13 of 13 rendered nodes.** `Badge.tsx:27-29` carries this comment:

> *"Uppercased by STYLE, not by rewriting the string. VoiceOver and TalkBack read short all-caps strings as initialisms — 'O-N H-O-L-D' — so the transform stays presentational and the accessible name keeps its natural case."*

On iOS RN 0.81 that is false: the transform is applied before the attributed string is built, so the accessible name **is** the transformed text. This app is iOS-only. There is no `accessibilityLabel={label}` at `Badge.tsx:30`, and `grep` for `textTransform|uppercase|VoiceOver|accessibilityLabel` across `src/__tests__/` returns **zero files** — no guard exists here. Affected: every status badge, every section title on three detail screens, `move.tsx`'s five form labels, `dashboard.tsx:105`, `onboarding-required.tsx:106`, `BoardCard.tsx:114`, `prospect.tsx:1049`.

**b. `accessibilityState` omits `disabled` on 17 elements that carry a `disabled` prop.** Sharpest: `deals/[id].tsx:222` *has* an `accessibilityState` that announces `{ selected }` and silently omits `disabled={watch.isPending}`; and `change-password.tsx:262` vs `login.tsx:126` — the *same shared control*, where login announces `{ disabled, busy }` and change-password announces nothing. `formStyles.ts:4-10` exists precisely because those two "were duplicated and had already drifted". They have drifted again, on accessibility this time, and the only disabled signal left is `opacity: 0.5`.

**c. The success and halt states of the highest-stakes action are silent.** `prospect.tsx:2008` (`savedBox`) and `:1932` (the duplicate picker) mount straight into the scroll with no `accessibilityRole="alert"`, no `announceForAccessibility`, no `scrollTo`. `change-password.tsx:78-95` already implements this correctly — including a note that `accessibilityLiveRegion` is Android-only and iOS needs `announceForAccessibility` — and applied it to a password-length hint. A VoiceOver rep taps Save and the app appears inert; in the halt case nothing was written.

**Fix.** `accessibilityLabel={label}` alongside every `textTransform` site (plus an AST guard test so it cannot regress); add the missing `disabled` keys; `accessibilityRole="alert"` + `announceForAccessibility` + `scrollTo` on the two prospect transitions, reusing the keyed-on-message pattern from `change-password.tsx:91-95`.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P1] The type scale reached four files, and two card components render one deal in two visual languages

**What.** `theme.type` defines 7 sizes (34/26/20/17/15/13/11) and is spread at **32 sites**. Against that: **151 raw `fontSize` literals outside `theme.ts`**, in **15 distinct values**, of which **69 occurrences (45.7%) are sizes the scale does not contain** — `14` alone appears 38 times. `dashboard.tsx`: 8 literals, **0** `theme.type` spreads. `theme.type.h1` has **zero uses** while `ScreenHeader.tsx:71` hand-rolls `fontSize: 26` + `font.bold` — which is what `h1` already is.

Concretely, on the most-opened screen: `DealCard.tsx:168-169` sets the deal name at 16 bold and the amount at **15** bold — a 1px difference between the two things a rep scans for. `BoardCard.tsx:163-169` renders the *same object* at `theme.type.title` (17) and `theme.type.h2` (20, `tabular-nums`) with a status rail. DealCard has no `tabular-nums`, so a scrolling column of amounts shimmers.

**Why it matters.** `theme.ts:161-168` documents that the scale exists because "a deal's name and its metadata carried nearly the same weight and nothing on screen led." That is still true on the card reached from Home and from the Deals tab.

**Fix.** Point `DealCard.name` at `theme.type.title` and `DealCard.amount` at `theme.type.h2` + `fontVariant: ["tabular-nums"]`. Better: delete `DealCard` and render `BoardCard` in the list — one fewer component, and guaranteed agreement between the two surfaces showing the same deals. Then work the 69 off-scale literals down.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable typeset`

### [P2] There is no mobile design authority on disk, and `DESIGN.md` now misdescribes half the product

**What.** `context.mjs` reports `surfaceBriefPath: null`. Repo-root `DESIGN.md` describes the light web client, and two of its named rules are contradicted by the shipping app: *"do not convert normal data cards into dark cards"* (every card in this app is a dark card) and *"Don't use border-left or border-right greater than 1px as a colored side stripe"* — contradicted literally by `BoardCard.tsx:155` `rail: { width: 3 }`.

Related mechanical drift: five comments across `ScreenHeader.tsx:60`, `LeadCard.tsx:101`, `DealCard.tsx:186`, `deals/[id].tsx:574`, `dashboard.tsx:216` still state ratios "on white" for a `surface` that is now `#14171C`. And `move.tsx:729` uses `theme.color.green` as type, which `theme.ts:136` reserves for "charts, dots, non-text" — it passes contrast at 7.88:1 by luck, so no test catches it; `greenText` exists for exactly this.

**Why it matters.** The divergence is *good* and the reasoning is sound, but it lives where no one running a brand audit will look. Design authority that describes a product you no longer ship stops being authority.

**Fix.** Add `mobile-crm/DESIGN.md` (or a `## Mobile surfaces` section) stating: the dark mandate and why; the two named-rule exemptions with their justification; `theme.ts` as the single token source; and the type scale as binding. One file converts 151 literals from "how it is" into "known debt".

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable document`

## Minor Observations

- Leads is the odd list out — `leads/index.tsx:147-157` uses its own inline header, so it is the only list with no brand mark and no office name; a multi-office rep cannot tell whose leads they are reading.
- `deals/[id].tsx:381` prints the raw activity type into the feed; `prospect.tsx:68-74` already holds the slug→label map.

Two theoretical gaps exist but are unreachable in current code — `textMuted` on `amberSurface` (3.85:1) and on `greenSurface` (4.09:1) — and `theme-contrast.test.ts` does not assert them. - No haptics anywhere — a committed capture, a stage move and a dedup halt are all silent, on a phone in a gloved hand in a noisy environment.
- Nine browser-only detector rules never executed and their status is **unknown**, not clean. They are all DOM-render rules with no RN meaning, so this is a formality rather than a gap.

## Questions to Consider

1. If the capture is why this app exists, why is it a route pushed off the Home tab rather than the fifth tab — or the launch destination whenever the rep is more than 100m from the office?
2. What if the visit attached to nothing? The screen spends 2,291 lines making sure an activity has a target. What if the field capture were a raw geotagged note, and property/company/contact resolution became the office's job — reconciled on a desktop with real data — rather than the rep's, at a door, on one bar?
3. Every write in this app is honest about being indeterminate. Why is none of them queued? A local outbox with a visible "3 waiting to send" converts the product's most-repeated moment of anxiety into a status line.
4. The board shows one column at a time because "a phone cannot show a desktop kanban". Is a board the right mobile object at all? The rep's actual question is "what needs me today" — and no screen answers it.
5. There are two card components for one deal. If the answer is `BoardCard`, the deals list should be *deleted*, not upgraded — you lose a component and gain consistency in the same commit.
6. Why is there no photo anywhere in a construction CRM? T-Rock Cam lives in the same repo and the rep carries one phone. A roof condition captured as a sentence is a sentence; captured as a photo it is evidence.
7. The type scale was authored to fix "nothing on screen leads" and reached four files. Is the remaining 22 a migration you intend to finish, or evidence the scale does not fit the denser screens — in which case, what does the deals list actually need?
8. `DESIGN.md` describes a light product that no longer exists on mobile. Who owns that document, and what happens the next time someone audits against it?
